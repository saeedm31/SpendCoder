SpendCoder · how it works

From a purchase line to a spend category

Accounting teams get thousands of purchase and invoice lines. Each one needs the right spend category. SpendCoder learns to do this from past examples, and it asks a person whenever it is not sure.

Follow one example line through every step: Annual maintenance contract for production line pumps
Records→ Clean & label→ Split by time→ Teach the model→ Test→ Package→ Use every day
1

Collect past purchase records

We start from real, public purchase records: contracts of the US Department of Energy, published on USAspending.gov. In a company, this would be your own invoice or purchase-order history.

Each record is a row in a table. A buyer wrote a short description and chose an official product or service code.

4

Teach the model: what a transformer does

the core idea

Think of hiring an experienced accountant who already knows English very well, then giving them a week of training on your own category list. The transformer is the experienced accountant; fine-tuning is the week of training.

1 · It cuts text into pieces

The sentence is split into small pieces (tokens), and each piece becomes numbers the model can work with.

annualmaintenancecontractforproductionlinepumps

2 · It reads words in context

It looks at all words at once and asks which ones matter to each other. "Pumps" on its own could be a product to buy; next to "maintenance" it means a repair service.

3 · It scores every category

At the end it gives each of the 14 categories a score. The scores add up to 100%: that is how sure it is.

Pre-trained, then fine-tuned

Pre-trained: the model (DistilRoBERTa, 82 million numbers inside) already read a huge amount of English text. It knows how language works, but nothing about spend categories.

Fine-tuned: we show it our 17,098 labelled lines a few times over. Each time it guesses, compares with the right answer and adjusts itself a little. About 15 minutes on one graphics card.

We also train a much simpler word-matching model as a yardstick. The transformer has to beat it to earn its place.

5

Test it honestly on the newest year

We ask both models about the 1,547 newest lines and count how often they pick the official category.

On this data the transformer and the simple model score about the same, and using both together scores best. Short, keyword-heavy lines are easy for word matching too. Knowing this before going live is the point of testing.

Share of test lines put in the right category
Simple word-matching model67.3%
Fine-tuned transformer66.5%
Both together (average)68.1%

Package it to run anywhere

The trained model is saved as one compact file and shrunk to a quarter of its size with no loss in quality. It runs on an ordinary server, no graphics card needed, and is stored with a description of how it was tested.

7

Use it every day, with a person in the loop

A new line arrives through the web page or straight from the accounting system. In about a second the model returns a category and how sure it is.

A confidence bar decides what happens next. We set it so that automatic codings are about 95% right on the check year. A higher bar means fewer mistakes and more lines for people.

Our example line, real answer from the live demo
Annual maintenance contract for production line pumps
Equipment maintenance & repair69.7%
The other 13 categories together30.3%
Is the model at least 99% sure?
Yes · coded automatically

The line is posted with its category. With this bar, 29% of test lines went this way, and 91% of them were right.

No · a person decides

The line goes to a review queue with the model's top 3 suggestions. Our pump line lands here: 69.7% is below the bar. Its top suggestion is the right category, so the check is quick.

↺ Back to step 2: every decision a person makes becomes a new labelled example. The model is retrained on them regularly, and a new version goes live only if it beats the current one on the same test.
